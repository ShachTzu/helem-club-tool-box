Owns the step between "registered an account" and "is a community member".

Every account that signs up — by email one-time code or by Google, it makes no
difference here — gets a `MemberProfile` row on its first authenticated page
load. The member fills the onboarding form once, which moves them to `pending`,
and an admin approves or declines them from `/admin/members`.

## States

| state | meaning | in Hebrew |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `none` | registered, onboarding not completed | לא חבר קהילה |
| `pending` | onboarding submitted, awaiting a decision | חבר קהילה ממתין לאישור |
| `approved` | an approved member | חבר קהילה |
| `rejected` | declined or revoked | לא חבר קהילה |

## Why a separate collection

The onboarding answers include an injury description and disability-recognition
status — health data, which is sensitive personal information under
חוק הגנת הפרטיות. Keeping it in its own collection with its own GraphQL types
means no public query, serializer or cache on the platform's `User` can leak it
by accident.

Two shapes, and only two:

- `MyMembership` — the state and the member's own name, readable by that member.
- `AdminMemberProfile` — the full answers, admin-gated on every resolver.

The owner of a profile always comes from the session, never from resolver input,
so a member cannot write onto anyone else's row.
